Integrated Cognitive Diagnostic

Brain and Mind Academy Psychometric Evaluation Suite

Complete the 3-step diagnostic assessment covering Howard Gardner's Multiple Intelligences, VAK Sensory Processing, and Cerebral Hemispheric Dominance to generate your comprehensive 20-page customized PDF report. Every selection is recorded with auditory feedback and turns green upon choice.

This is a diagnostic self-assessment, not a test. There are no right or wrong answers, no pass or fail, and no time limit. Its only purpose is to help you understand how you naturally think, learn and process information.

What This Assessment Measures

The suite combines three well-established, independent frameworks so the final report reflects a rounded picture of the student's cognitive profile rather than a single score.

1. Multiple Intelligences

64 statements across 8 sections (A–H), rated on how true each one feels for you.

~15–18 minutes

2. VAK Learning Style

47 real-life scenarios — pick the option (A, B or C) that best matches your instinctive reaction.

~12–15 minutes

3. Brain Dominance

21 paired statements — choose the one (A or B) that sounds more like you.

~8–10 minutes

How & When to Attempt This Assessment

Follow these guidelines so the results genuinely reflect the student's natural tendencies rather than a rushed or overthought response.

Best time: a calm, unhurried moment — not right before or after an exam, and not when tired or distracted.

Environment: a quiet space, free of interruptions, so responses aren't influenced by others.

Go with your first instinct. The first answer that comes to mind is usually the most accurate — avoid overanalyzing each statement.

Answer as yourself, not as you'd like to be seen. There is no "ideal" profile — every combination has real strengths.

No time limit, but try to finish all three sections in one sitting (roughly 35–45 minutes total) for consistent context.

It can be retaken later — every 1–2 years is reasonable — to see how interests and preferences evolve over time.

Learning Style Pattern

My Learning Style (VAK Profile)

Your learning styles have more influence than you may realize. Your preferred styles guide the way you learn. They also change the way you internally represent experiences, the way you recall information, and even the words you choose. Research shows us that each learning style uses different parts of the brain. By involving more of the brain during learning, we remember more of what we learn.

Visual Learner

Visual learners learn best by seeing.

  • • They like to read silently.
  • • They enjoy visual stimulation, colors, vivid imagery.
  • • Relate most effectively to written information.
  • • They can remember faces but forget names.

Auditory Learner

Auditory learners learn best by listening.

  • • They like to read out loud.
  • • They are good at grammar and foreign language.
  • • They notice sound effects in movies.
  • • They can't keep quiet for long periods.
  • • They are not afraid to speak in class.

Kinesthetic Learner

Kinesthetic learners learn best by doing.

  • • They are generally good at sports/outdoor activities.
  • • Respond while practically doing things rather than listening/reading.
  • • They enjoy dancing while listening to music.
  • • They like adventure books or movies.

Cerebral Hemispheric Balance

Brain Dominance Assessment

Left Brain Dominance

  1. Analytical brain: More inclined towards self-awareness, logical thinking, fine motor skills activities, language & grammar and may have a hidden love towards nature.
  2. These people are generally good in academics.
  3. They have convergent thinking and can bring their energy and focus at one point.
  4. They prefer to respond to verbal instructions.
  5. They like to solve the problems by looking at the parts of things.
  6. They are able locate the differences in similar things easily.

Right Brain Dominance

  1. Creative brain: More inclined towards interpersonal skills, imagination, gross motor skills activities, music, colors, pictures, dance, art, rhythms, acting, painting, modelling, fashion, outdoor sports etc.
  2. They are generally good in extracurricular activities, primarily creative ones.
  3. They tend to throw the rules out of window.
  4. They have divergent thinking which is full of creativity and ideas.
  5. They are often lost in their own ideas, thoughts and world. They may be physically present, mentally absent.
